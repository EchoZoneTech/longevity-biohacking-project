### SECTION 1: WHAT IS NAD+ AND WHY IT MATTERS (1:30 - 3:00)

**Visual:** Cellular animation showing NAD+ in action, mitochondria graphics
**Voiceover:**
"NAD+ stands for Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide - yes, it's a mouthful, which is why everyone just calls it NAD+.

Think of NAD+ as the cellular currency for energy production. Every single cell in your body needs NAD+ to convert the food you eat into usable energy. But that's just the beginning.

**NAD+ has three critical functions:**

**1. Energy Production:** NAD+ is essential for your mitochondria - the powerhouses of your cells - to create ATP, which is the energy molecule your body runs on.

**2. DNA Repair:** Your DNA gets damaged thousands of times per day from normal metabolism, UV radiation, and environmental stressors. NAD+ activates enzymes called PARPs that repair this damage.

**3. Sirtuin Activation:** NAD+ activates a family of longevity genes called sirtuins. These genes regulate inflammation, cellular stress response, and metabolic health. They're basically your body's anti-aging defense system.

Without adequate NAD+, these critical processes slow down - and that's exactly what happens as we age."

### SECTION 2: THE NAD+ DECLINE PROBLEM (3:00 - 5:15)

**Visual:** Graph showing NAD+ decline with age, before/after comparisons
**Voiceover:**
"Here's the problem: NAD+ levels plummet as we get older. Studies show that by age 50, you have roughly 50% less NAD+ than you did at age 20. By age 80, some tissues have less than 10% of youthful levels.

**Why does this happen?**

Three main reasons:

**1. Decreased Production:** Your body becomes less efficient at making NAD+ from dietary precursors.

**2. Increased Consumption:** Cellular damage accumulates with age, using up more NAD+ for DNA repair.

**3. Enzyme Degradation:** An enzyme called CD38 increases with age and breaks down NAD+ faster.

**What does low NAD+ mean for you?**

Research published in Cell Metabolism and Nature Reviews shows that NAD+ decline is associated with:
- Reduced mitochondrial function and energy
- Impaired DNA repair and cellular damage
- Decreased sirtuin activity
- Accelerated aging processes
- Higher risk of age-related diseases including metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, and neurodegeneration

The good news? Multiple strategies can restore NAD+ levels, and the research is incredibly promising."

### SECTION 4: NMN VS NR - WHICH SUPPLEMENT IS BETTER? (7:45 - 9:30)

**Visual:** Molecular structures comparison, supplement bottles, absorption pathway
**Voiceover:**
"Now let's tackle the big question: If you decide to supplement, should you take NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) or NR (Nicotinamide Riboside)?

Both are precursors to NAD+ - meaning your body converts them into NAD+. But there are some key differences.

**NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide):**
- One step closer to NAD+ in the conversion pathway
- Slightly larger molecule
- May have a specific transporter for direct cell entry
- More research in animal models
- Typically dosed at 250-500mg daily
- Generally more expensive

**NR (Nicotinamide Riboside):**
- Two steps away from NAD+ conversion
- Smaller molecule, easier cellular absorption proven
- More human clinical trials published
- FDA GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) status
- Typically dosed at 300-500mg daily
- Usually more affordable

**The honest answer?** Both work. Both successfully raise NAD+ levels in human studies. The 'best' choice often comes down to:
- Your budget
- Individual response (some people respond better to one vs. the other)
- Availability and quality of the specific brand

My recommendation? Try one for 2-3 months, track how you feel and any measurable changes, then you can experiment with the other if you want to compare.

**Quality matters:** Look for products with third-party testing, purity verification, and good manufacturing practices."

### SECTION 5: NATURAL WAYS TO BOOST NAD+ (9:30 - 11:00)

**Visual:** Food sources, exercise footage, fasting graphics
**Voiceover:**
"Before we talk dosing, let's cover natural ways to boost NAD+ - because supplements aren't the only option.

**1. Exercise:**
Both resistance training and cardiovascular exercise increase NAD+ levels naturally. High-intensity interval training (HIIT) is particularly effective.

**2. Fasting and Caloric Restriction:**
Intermittent fasting and time-restricted eating boost NAD+ production. Even a 12-16 hour fast can have benefits.

**3. Heat Exposure:**
Sauna use and heat stress activate heat shock proteins and support NAD+ production. Aim for 20-30 minutes at 170-185°F, 3-4 times weekly.

**4. NAD+ Boosting Foods:**
While you can't eat NAD+ directly, certain foods contain precursors:
- Cow's milk (contains NR naturally)
- Fish - particularly tuna and salmon
- Mushrooms
- Green vegetables - broccoli, cabbage, cucumber
- Whole grains
- Fermented foods

**5. Minimize NAD+ Depletion:**
- Limit alcohol consumption (alcohol metabolism uses NAD+)
- Reduce excessive sun exposure (causes DNA damage requiring NAD+ for repair)
- Manage chronic stress
- Get adequate sleep (supports NAD+ restoration)

**6. Support the Methylation Cycle:**
B vitamins, particularly B12, folate, and B6, support the pathways that recycle and produce NAD+."

### SECTION 6: DOSING, TIMING, AND SAFETY (11:00 - 12:30)

**Visual:** Dosing charts, supplement timing graphics, safety considerations
**Voiceover:**
"If you decide to supplement with NMN or NR, here's what the research suggests:

**Typical Dosing:**
- NMN: 250-500mg daily, some protocols go up to 1000mg
- NR: 300-500mg daily, therapeutic studies use 1000mg

**Timing:**
Take in the morning on an empty stomach for best absorption. NAD+ is involved in circadian rhythm, so morning dosing may support natural energy cycles.

**What to Expect:**
Most people notice effects within 2-4 weeks:
- Increased energy
- Better mental clarity
- Improved exercise recovery
- Better sleep quality

**Safety Considerations:**

Current research shows NMN and NR are well-tolerated with minimal side effects. Human trials up to 1000mg daily show good safety profiles.

**Potential mild side effects:**
- Nausea (usually from taking on empty stomach)
- Flushing or warmth
- Headache in first few days

**Who should be cautious:**
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women (insufficient research)
- People with cancer history (consult oncologist - NAD+ supports cell growth)
- Those on medications (check for interactions)

**Always consult your doctor** before starting any new supplement, especially if you have medical conditions or take medications.

**Cycling:** Some protocols recommend cycling - like 5 days on, 2 days off - but long-term human data doesn't show this is necessary."
